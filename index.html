How it works

The outlook

The National Significant Wildland Fire Potential Outlook is issued on (or within the first few workdays of) the first of each month. Month 1 is the month it's issued, Month 2 the next, and Months 3 and 4 the extended outlook. Each covers the whole calendar month, PSA by PSA: Above Normal Below normal significant fire potential. Every night the site reads the PSA categories from the NPSG Outlooks_Monthly_Extended service (the same product as the NIFC maps) and saves the four NIFC map images with them. A new issue is saved the night it appears; a correction replaces it and is logged.

What counts as observed

A significant fire is a wildfire that reached 100 acres (each GACC can change this), counted in the PSA where it started and the month it was discovered. Fire records come from the NIFC WFIGS incident data (all years). Normal is the same calendar month in the previous 10 years. A month is Above when the count is above the top third of those years (and at least 2 fires), Below when it's under the bottom third, and Normal otherwise. Where a PSA almost never has a significant fire that month, the bottom third is 0 and Below can't happen; the site marks these. Acres burned by those fires are judged the same way and shown beside the count for comparison, but the count decides.

A month is in progress until it ends, provisional for 30 days after (sizes settle and late reports come in), then final.

Scores

Correct = same category. One category off = Above or Below against Normal. Opposite = Above against Below. Heidke skill score compares percent correct with what chance would get from the same mix of forecasts and outcomes (1 = perfect, 0 = no better than chance, below 0 = worse). The always-Normal score shows how a forecast of Normal everywhere would have done.

Weather and station checks

Every night the site saves each RAWS's daily max temperature, min RH, max wind and gust, rain total, and daily max ERC and BI (FEMS, observed values, each GACC's NFDRS 2016 fuel model), and the same weather from ASOS/AWOS airport stations (Iowa Environmental Mesonet, with the NCEI normal high and normal rain for each date). Each station's ERC is also compared with its own 80th, 90th and 97th percentiles.

Each station is checked against its nearest neighbors every day, so one bad sensor doesn't drive a PSA's numbers:

  • Too hot / too cold: the max temp is compared with the median of the nearest stations (within the GACC's distance and elevation limits), each adjusted to the station's elevation. More than 8°F (or 3 robust standard deviations) warmer is flagged hot; colder is cold.
  • Rain no neighbor saw: half an inch or more when no neighbor got more than 0.05".
  • Far more rain than any neighbor: an inch or more and over 4 times the wettest neighbor.
  • Missed rain: nothing when the neighbors' median was half an inch or more (a clogged, frozen or broken gauge).
  • Impossible values (negative or huge rain, temps outside −60 to 135°F).

Flagged values are left out of the PSA numbers but kept, with the reason, on the Weather & station checks tab. Over a month, a station hot (or cold) on 5 or more days is marked runs hot (runs cold), and one whose rain total is 3 times its neighbors' (or a third of it) reads wet (reads dry); those are left out of that month's PSA numbers too. PSA rain is the median of good stations' monthly totals; rain and temperature are compared with normal only at ASOS/AWOS sites, which have NCEI normals. RAWS rain gauges aren't heated, so winter snow is undercounted.

Data sources (public, no login)

  • Outlook categories: NPSG Outlooks_Monthly_Extended map service; maps: NIFC month1_outlook.png to month4_outlook.png.
  • Fires: NIFC WFIGS Incident Locations (all years, current and year-to-date).
  • RAWS daily weather, ERC, BI and ERC percentiles: FEMS Climatology API.
  • ASOS/AWOS daily summaries and normals: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
  • Map background: Esri Light Gray / Dark Gray Canvas (Esri, HERE, Garmin, OpenStreetMap contributors).
